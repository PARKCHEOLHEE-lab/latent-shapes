Latent Shapes

The trained SDF decoder, exported to ONNX and run on WebGPU by onnxruntime-web, reconstructing meshes coarse-to-fine live in browser

A latent shape — 98 vertices you can drag in 3D — encodes each chair; the trained decoder turns it into a signed-distance field, marching cubes polygonizes that field into a mesh, and a coarse-to-fine sweep refines only the cells near the surface, so the result appears within a second.

Limitations & Future Works

The main limitation is that the latent-shape vertices don't understand shapes at the part level. Each of the 98 vertices is a purely geometric handle over a nearby region of space — none maps to a part a person would name (a backrest, an armrest, a leg). So editing one part is indirect: you find which vertices affect it by trial and error, and a single drag can also bend nearby parts you didn't mean to touch.

A promising next step is making the latent shape part-aware — aligning vertices (or groups of them) with part labels such as PartNet, or learning the grouping directly, so grabbing “the armrest” moves exactly the vertices that encode it. That would turn space-level dragging into part-level editing.
